Been watching Magnus Carlsen for years. The man sees a chessboard differently than everyone else. You watch him make a move that looks completely ordinary, then ten moves later you realize the other guy has been walking into a cage the entire time.

That's the kind of thinking I respect.

Anyway, I was watching one of his matches from this year where he opened with the English Opening and something clicked.

Why not turn the game itself into a cipher?

I already had the first part built. Took a chessboard and mapped the squares to alphabetic characters. Obviously there are 64 squares and only 26 letters, so there is some repetition in the mapping. That is intentional. The square alone isn't enough.

Only Magnus's moves matter though. idk about the other player.

I used that particular 2026 Carlsen game to derive the key for a message I left behind on one of our victim's machines.

A chess game is public information. Nobody looks twice at it. That's what I like about this.

Millions of people can see the key material and have absolutely no idea they're looking at it.
